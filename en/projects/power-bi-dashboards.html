Power BI dashboards

01

The question

A fixed budget, three equipment offers, a sales file and a customer database: before deciding, the offers must be compared on quantity and price, then the data must show who buys, where and when.

The goal: produce readable visuals that help decide, then group them into dashboards, from two types of source, Excel files and a MySQL database.

02

The approach

  1. 1 · Power Query

    Import and clean

    Imported the Excel files, removed empty columns and checked each column’s type before plotting anything.

  2. 2 · Power BI

    Compare the offers

    Quantities by product and by offer, with HP ruled out for its low quantities, then total price against a budget line at 220,000 MAD.

  3. 3 · Power BI

    Tell the sales story

    Age groups, trend over time, split by gender and a city map with a gradient from red (minimum) to green (maximum).

  4. 4 · Power Query

    Fix what skews results

    A misspelled city, which would be misplaced on the map, and a date that does not exist: corrected for one, removed for the other.

  5. 5 · MySQL

    Connect to a database

    ODBC connection to a local MySQL database, table selection and conversion of a date stored as text.

  6. 6 · Power BI

    Assemble the dashboards

    Visuals grouped by page, clear titles and a “top 10” filter to rank the best customers.

03

The dashboards

Three views, each answering one question: which offer to choose, how products sell, who the customers are.

05

Takeaways

Method

Clean before showing

A column type or a misspelled city is enough to skew a chart: checks come first, in Power Query.

Limitation

Demonstration data

The datasets are examples: conclusions describe the observed figures, not a real company situation.

Scope: demonstration datasets, used as a simulated business case. The dashboards describe the observed figures; they do not establish causes.
